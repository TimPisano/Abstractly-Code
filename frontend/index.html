Why Multifamily Is Different

Built For Hundreds Of Units, Not A Handful Of Tenants

Most lease-review tools were built for commercial CRE — a handful of long, heavily negotiated leases per building. Multifamily is a different problem entirely.

Volume, not negotiation

A mid-size portfolio can mean hundreds of similar residential leases, not a dozen heavily negotiated commercial ones. Checking that by hand doesn't scale — and a tool built to review 15 anchor tenants a year wasn't built for 300 units.

Constant turnover

Unit leases renew and turn over year-round across a portfolio, not on a handful of scheduled dates. The rent roll drifts from the actual leases continuously — not occasionally — which is exactly why it needs checking before every acquisition, not just once a year.

Built for the closing table, not asset management

This isn't a tool for an owner managing a handful of tenants over years. It's for the diligence window before you sign — checking what the seller's rent roll claims against what the leases actually say, while you can still walk or reprice.

The Report

What It Catches

Every finding is a specific disagreement between the rent roll and a signed lease for the same unit — not a statistical outlier, not a guess. Each one links back to the page and quote in the lease.

Rent that doesn't match the lease

The rent roll shows one base rent; the lease says another. Flagged with the monthly dollar gap, so a $1,550/mo difference sorts above a rounding artifact.

A tenant who was replaced

The name on the rent roll isn't the entity on the signed lease — an assignment or a new lease the management system never caught up to.

A stale expiration date

The rent roll still shows the pre-renewal expiration while the executed lease was extended — the single most common form of stale property-management data.

Escalations and dates that don't add up

Rent-step math that doesn't reconcile, conflicting start/end dates, missing clauses — the details that change an underwriting model but hide in the document.

Platform

Built For The Team That Checks The Rent Roll By Hand

Three things a rent roll needs before it's actually usable — standardized structure, real validation, and analysis you can underwrite from. Items marked "Coming soon" are on our roadmap, not live yet.

Standardization

  • ✓ Pulls tenant, rent, dates, escalations, and renewal terms out of lease PDFs — including scanned copies, using text recognition (OCR)
  • ✓ Automatically splits a PDF containing multiple leases into separate, accurate records
  • ✓ Import a broker-built Excel or CSV rent roll — no fixed column format required, we match your headers automatically, skip vacant/total rows, and give every value the same page/row-level citation a PDF-extracted field gets
  • ✓ Direct import from Yardi, AppFolio, RealPage, MRI, and Buildium rent roll exports — auto-detects each report's real header row past any title/date block, uses the tenant's actual rent (never a "Market Rent" column or a per-square-foot rate), and correctly handles portfolio-wide exports spanning multiple properties in one file. Validated against realistic synthetic sample exports built to match each platform's real report structure; not yet run against a real customer file from any of them
The Wedge

Validation

  • ✓ Every value links back to the exact page and quote it came from — click any citation to check it yourself, nothing is a black-box guess
  • ✓ Automatically flags anything unusual: below-market rent, missing clauses, odd notice periods, escalation math that doesn't add up, conflicting dates
  • ✓ Cross-checks the rent roll against your T12 (trailing 12-month operating statement) — compares the rent roll's own annualized rent against the T12's actual collected income (never a "Gross Potential Rent" line, which would make a perfectly healthy property look short by definition) for the same property, flagging a real gap. Validated against a realistic synthetic sample statement; not yet run against a real customer T12
  • ✓ Cross-checks an imported rent roll against the actual lease documents on file for the same units, flagging tenant, rent, and expiration-date disagreements — catches stale property-management data a renewal never made it back into

Analysis

  • ✓ Portfolio totals — units, square footage, total rent, and average rent per square foot
  • ✓ Tracks lease expirations and renewal deadlines in 30/60/90-day windows — kept separate, since they're not the same date
  • ✓ Loss-to-lease analysis — the upside per unit against the best rent already proven elsewhere in the same building. An internal comp, not external market data (we don't have a market-rent feed yet) — a property with only one unit on file has no comp and is excluded rather than guessed at

FAQ

Questions We'd Ask Too

What happens to our data after we upload it?

Your documents and the data extracted from them are stored under your account and are never shared with, or resold to, a third party. You can delete any document and its extracted data at any time. If you export to Google Sheets, that data leaves our system and lives in your own Google account, under Google's own terms — we don't control it once it's there.

How accurate is the extraction?

We're not going to hand you a made-up accuracy percentage — anyone can print a number, and it means nothing without knowing what it was measured against. What we do instead: every extracted value shows a confidence level and links back to the exact page and quote it came from, so you (or your analyst) can verify any figure in seconds rather than trusting a black box. In our own internal testing, a genuine 40-page lease document processed in 0.06 seconds — extraction speed isn't the bottleneck; verifiability is the point.

What file formats and property management systems do you support?

Today: PDF lease documents, including scanned/image-only PDFs (via OCR), including files that bundle multiple leases into one document, plus hand-built broker Excel/CSV rent rolls and direct import from Yardi, AppFolio, RealPage, MRI, and Buildium exports — see the Platform section above for the full accuracy caveats on each.

How is this different from an analyst just eyeballing the rent roll for outliers?

Outlier flagging against portfolio averages is table stakes, and we do that too. The actual difference is validating against source documents, not just internal statistics — every value we surface traces back to the original lease itself, and cross-checking a rent roll against both the T12 and the underlying lease documents (not just against itself) is the real differentiator, not a nice-to-have layered on top.

Does this replace my analyst?

No. It removes the slowest, most error-prone part of their job — manually retyping and cross-referencing a rent roll line by line — so they spend their time on judgment calls: is this deal worth pursuing, not did I transpose a rent figure correctly.
