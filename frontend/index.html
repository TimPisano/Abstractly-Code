Platform

Built For The Analyst Who Reviews These By Hand

Three things a rent roll needs before it's actually usable — standardized structure, real validation, and analysis you can underwrite from. Items marked "Coming soon" are on our roadmap, not live yet.

Standardization

  • ✓ Pulls tenant, rent, dates, escalations, and renewal terms out of lease PDFs — including scanned copies, using text recognition (OCR)
  • ✓ Automatically splits a PDF containing multiple leases into separate, accurate records
  • ✓ Import a broker-built Excel or CSV rent roll — no fixed column format required, we match your headers automatically, skip vacant/total rows, and give every value the same page/row-level citation a PDF-extracted field gets
  • Coming soon Direct import from Yardi, AppFolio, RealPage, MRI, and Buildium exports specifically
The Wedge

Validation

  • ✓ Every value links back to the exact page and quote it came from — click any citation to check it yourself, nothing is a black-box guess
  • ✓ Automatically flags anything unusual: below-market rent, missing clauses, odd notice periods, escalation math that doesn't add up, conflicting dates
  • Coming soon Cross-checks the rent roll against your T12 (trailing 12-month operating statement) — not just portfolio averages
  • ✓ Cross-checks an imported rent roll against the actual lease documents on file for the same units, flagging tenant, rent, and expiration-date disagreements — catches stale property-management data a renewal never made it back into

Analysis

  • ✓ Portfolio totals — units, square footage, total rent, and average rent per square foot
  • ✓ Tracks lease expirations and renewal deadlines in 30/60/90-day windows — kept separate, since they're not the same date
  • ✓ Tenant concentration analysis — how much of your rent depends on your largest tenants, with an automatic high/moderate/low read (Herfindahl-Hirschman Index, the same standard used to measure market concentration)
  • ✓ WALT (weighted average lease term, weighted by rent) and a year-by-year rollover schedule — how much rent expires each year, with a high/moderate/low risk read on how concentrated your near-term rollover is
  • ✓ Loss-to-lease analysis — the upside per unit against the best rent already proven elsewhere in the same building. An internal comp, not external market data (we don't have a market-rent feed yet) — a property with only one unit on file has no comp and is excluded rather than guessed at

FAQ

Questions We'd Ask Too

What happens to our data after we upload it?

Your documents and the data extracted from them are stored under your account and are never shared with, or resold to, a third party. You can delete any document and its extracted data at any time. If you export to Google Sheets, that data leaves our system and lives in your own Google account, under Google's own terms — we don't control it once it's there.

How accurate is the extraction?

We're not going to hand you a made-up accuracy percentage — anyone can print a number, and it means nothing without knowing what it was measured against. What we do instead: every extracted value shows a confidence level and links back to the exact page and quote it came from, so you (or your analyst) can verify any figure in seconds rather than trusting a black box. In our own internal testing, a genuine 40-page lease document processed in 0.06 seconds — extraction speed isn't the bottleneck; verifiability is the point.

What file formats and property management systems do you support?

Today: PDF lease documents, including scanned/image-only PDFs (via OCR), including files that bundle multiple leases into one document. Direct ingestion of rent roll exports from Yardi, AppFolio, RealPage, MRI, Buildium, and hand-built broker Excel/CSV files is on our roadmap — see the Platform section above for exactly what's live versus coming soon.

How is this different from an analyst just eyeballing the rent roll for outliers?

Outlier flagging against portfolio averages is table stakes, and we do that too. The actual difference is validating against source documents, not just internal statistics — every value we surface traces back to the original lease itself, and cross-checking a rent roll against the T12 and the underlying leases (not just against itself) is the capability we're building toward as the real differentiator, not a nice-to-have layered on top.

Does this replace my analyst?

No. It removes the slowest, most error-prone part of their job — manually retyping and cross-referencing a rent roll line by line — so they spend their time on judgment calls: is this deal worth pursuing, not did I transpose a rent figure correctly.
